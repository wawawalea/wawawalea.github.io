When studying a machine learning problem, I like to ask:

1. What is the object of study?
Before I measure or optimize something, I ask what it actually is.

Often the real question is whether the thing we probe is a single object at all, or a loose set of case-specific cues.

2. How does it work?
Once the object is clear, I ask what produces it.

I want the mechanism and the learning dynamics behind a behavior, not just whether a metric moves.

3. How far does it transfer?
A result on one setting is not a general result. I ask what transfers, and where it breaks.

In continual and post-training work, the question is not whether something changed. It is how far the change transfers across tasks, models, and distributions.
