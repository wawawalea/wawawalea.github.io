When studying a machine learning problem, I like to ask:

1. What is the object of the study?
Before measuring or optimizing something, I want to know what the thing actually is.

Sometimes the hardest part of a project is deciding whether the thing we are probing or optimizing is a real object at all.

2. How does it work?
Once an object is in view, I want to understand the dynamics that produce or use it.

I am interested in the mechanisms and learning dynamics behind a behavior, not just whether a metric moves.

3. How do we study its adaptation, and to what extent?
A local success is not yet a general one; I want to know what carries over, and where it breaks.
